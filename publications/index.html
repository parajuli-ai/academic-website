Neither Blinding Nor a Jury, Neither Capability Nor Strategy: What Brings a Verifier to Reliability

J. Kozman, A. Waddepalli, T. Parajuli · NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Trust-AI-Eval (TAE) · Accepted; extended version under review at ICLR 2027

Across 64,800 controlled verifications, a model approves its own wrong answers 11.9 percentage points more often than other models do, and telling it who wrote the answer changes almost nothing. Only execution grounding restores reliability: a grounded judge catches 93.3% of errors against 76.7% for the best ungrounded jury.

My contribution: mathematics domain; prompt-parity test that keeps verifier strategy a clean independent variable.

Which Verifier Catches What? Mapping Error Severity to Verifier Strategy in Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines

T. Parajuli, J. Kozman, A. Waddepalli · SISTER 2026, selected as a top AI/ML project · Manuscript

Controlled fault injection into a plan/solve/verify pipeline. Removing the verifier raises execution-stage error propagation to 27.3%; chain-of-thought and rubric verifiers repair 95 to 98% of the faults they catch, against 78.5% for direct prompting.

My contribution: First author. Design, experiments, analysis.
